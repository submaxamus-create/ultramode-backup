## 2. Model overview — Aluma 8216 Tilt

The 8216 Tilt is Aluma's all‑aluminum, tandem‑axle, **tilt‑bed** car hauler / open utility
trailer. The "8216" naming = **82" wide bed × 16' long deck**. The bed pivots so the rear
drops to the ground, letting you drive low cars, mowers, ATVs, scissor lifts, etc. straight
on — no separate ramps needed. This design has been essentially unchanged from the
2016‑era unit you have through the current (2026/2027) model years, so modern spec sheets
apply closely to your trailer.

### Core specifications

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Bed width | 82" (overall width ~101.5" over fenders) |
| Deck length | 16' |
| GVWR | 7,000 lb |
| Axles | 2 × 3,500 lb rubber **torsion** axles, easy‑lube hubs |
| Brakes | Electric brakes + breakaway kit |
| Empty weight | ~1,425–1,650 lb depending on options/year |
| Payload | ~5,300–5,575 lb |
| Tires | ST205/75R14 LRC radial (~1,760 lb/tire) on aluminum wheels |
| Tilt angle | ~7.5°–11° (load) with controlled rate of descent |

### Standard features
- **Hydraulic dampener with gas lift** controlling the tilt — adjustable rate of descent;
  this is the cylinder visible in your underside photo. It is a *cushioned gravity tilt*,
  not a powered hydraulic pump.
- **Bed locks** — secure the bed for travel and hold it in the up position.
- Extruded aluminum floor; front (and on some years side) retaining rail.
- Removable aluminum teardrop fenders with fender steps.
- 44.5" A‑framed aluminum tongue with a **2‑5/16" coupler**.
- Stake pockets, LED lighting package, safety chains.
- All‑aluminum construction → corrosion resistant, light tow weight.

## 3. Tilt mechanism — what's in your photo 3

The underside photo shows the **hydraulic dampener cylinder** and pivot hardware. On the
8216 Tilt this is a sealed gas/hydraulic cushion unit, not a powered ram. Operation:

1. Release the bed lock.
2. Weight (or you pushing) tips the bed; the rear deck lowers to grade.
3. The dampener controls the descent so the bed doesn't slam down.
4. After loading, the bed returns level and the lock re‑engages for travel.

**Maintenance watch‑items** (consistent with the wear/rust visible on the pivot pins in the
photo): keep the pivot pins and lock mechanism greased, verify the dampener still cushions
the drop (a worn unit lets the bed fall fast — a safety issue), and check the bed‑lock
engages fully before towing.

## 3a. Troubleshooting — "deck only tilts down halfway" (logged 2026-06-25)

**Symptom observed on this unit:** With the two front latches released and the petcock
opened, the empty deck tilts down only ~halfway and stalls. Standing on the very rear and
jumping forces it the rest of the way to the ground.

**Why this happens (the mechanism):** This is a *balanced gravity tilt* with a hydraulic
dampener + gas spring. The whole hydraulic/gas unit is bought from **Force America**; Aluma
has no manual or parts diagram for it.
- The **two front latches** lock the deck level for travel.
- The **petcock** is a bleed valve on the dampener cylinder: open = oil free to transfer
  (deck can move), closed = hydraulically locked. It controls *whether/how fast* the deck
  moves, **not how far**.
- The **gas spring + the deck's forward‑of‑center balance** are what hold the bed up. Empty,
  the deck wants to sit level; it's tuned to tilt **under load** and to help self‑raise.
- Empty, the only thing tilting it down is the short rear overhang's weight, working against
  the gas spring. It **stalls at the force‑balance point**; jumping on the tail adds enough
  moment to push past it. Partly by design — it's meant to drop the rest of the way as a
  load rolls on.

**But this much resistance isn't normal.** Likely causes, in order:
1. **Dry/rusty pivot pins (most likely — visible in the underside photo).** Stiction eats the
   marginal empty tilting force. **Fix: clean and grease the pivot pins/bushings,** retry
   empty. Often resolves it on its own.
2. **Gas spring charge/condition.** After ~10 years it may bind or be mis‑charged. Some units
   have a **Schrader air fitting on the hydraulic tube** to adjust the charge — *but lowering
   it to drop easier can make the deck slam and fail to self‑return.* Only adjust to the
   supplier's spec.
3. **Petcock not fully open / old sludgy oil** restricting flow (slows rather than stops).
4. **Mechanical catch** — debris between deck and frame, or a tweaked retaining rail at the
   mid‑tilt position.

**Safety:** A "stuck" tilt deck can break free suddenly. Do not stand under it or put hands
at pinch points; don't load/unload until it cycles smoothly. The jump‑on‑the‑end trick is
exactly how people get caught when it lets go.

**Recommended order:** (1) clean/grease pivots → retry empty. (2) If still stuck, the gas
unit needs service/recharge — call **Aluma 1‑866‑415‑3285 with the last 6 of VIN (160851)**
and ask them to identify the **Force America** hydraulic/gas unit, then have a hydraulics
shop service it.

## 3b. Grease / pivot-point map for freeing up the tilt

**Important reality:** Aluma tilt decks have **NO grease zerks on the tilt mechanism.** The
only zerk fittings on the whole trailer are the **two EZ‑Lube wheel hubs** (axle bearings) —
unrelated to the tilt. The tilt pivots are **steel pins in aluminum/steel brackets**, run
dry from the factory, so they suffer **galvanic corrosion** (steel + aluminum). You free
them by **cleaning the pins and lubricating them directly** — there is nothing to pump.

**Friction points to service (≈6–8 pivots, none with zerks), in priority order:**
1. **Two main deck pivot pins (MOST important).** One each side, on the hinge axis where the
   tilting deck rotates on the frame — located **behind the axles toward the rear of the
   deck**. Carries the whole deck load; corrosion here stalls the tilt.
2. **Hydraulic cylinder clevis pins** — the badly rusted pins visible in the underside photo
   (left rod‑end pin stack + right‑side clevis pin). 2–4 pins.
3. **Intermediate linkage pins** between the cylinder and the deck (the center bracket with
   the hole in the photo).
4. **Two front latch / bed‑lock pivots** — must swing fully free so a latch can't drag or
   partially re‑engage.

> The **yellow‑capped fitting on the right** of the underside photo should be identified
> before touching — it may be the **air/charge valve** for the gas side, or a hydraulic
> fitting/bleeder.

**How to free them (no zerk = clean-and-lube method):**
1. **Penetrant first** — PB Blaster / Kroil on every pin; soak, work the deck, re‑soak.
2. **Wire-brush** the rust off the exposed steel pins.
3. **Lube for dissimilar metals** — use **anti‑seize** at steel‑in‑aluminum joints (better
   long‑term than grease, which washes out); marine waterproof grease also acceptable.
4. **Seized pin?** Drive it out, clean the bore, reinstall with anti‑seize — the permanent
   fix for galvanic lock‑up.
5. **Cycle the empty deck** several times after lubing before trusting it.

_Note: main rear hinge-pin location is inferred from Aluma 8200-series construction + the
underside photo; confirm by watching where the deck pivots and where it sticks._

## 3c. Problems & Fixes catalog (Aluma tilt / aluminum trailers)

Collected from Aluma docs, dealer sheets, and owner forums (Garage Journal, OrangeTractorTalks,
Mobile Abode, etrailer). The hydraulic/gas tilt unit is made by **Force America**, rated to
**~2,500 psi**; it's a **closed-loop** system that uses the lower side-to-side cylinder for oil
storage (extended when the deck is flat, retracted when tilted). Aluma stocks no manual or
parts diagram for it — Force America is the support path.

| # | Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deck only tilts partway down** (this unit) | Galvanic/dry pivot stiction; gas charge too strong; design needs rear load | Clean + anti‑seize pivots (§3b); service/recharge gas unit; load tilts it the rest of the way. See §3a |
| 2 | **Deck won't return up / rises weakly** | Weak/worn gas shock (small shock raises, big cylinder dampens descent) | Gas shocks are easily replaceable; **adding an extra gas shock** improves lift — owners report it then holds tilted even loaded |
| 3 | **Deck slams down / no cushion; "dead spot" mid‑travel** | Dampener low on oil or air‑bound; known low‑damping zone in the stroke | Refill fluid + **purge air via the quick‑connects**, cycle up/down several times; recharge gas side; replace dampener if spent |
| 4 | **Deck won't stay down in load position** | Closed‑loop hand (ball) valve not holding | Use the hand valve to hold position; one owner found it won't hold with the ball valve fully closed — verify the valve seats; use **bed locks** to hold the *up* position |
| 5 | **Hydraulic cylinder leaking oil** | Wetness at bottom fittings; **hose rubbing on a fitting** wears it; bad O‑ring | Re‑route/secure the hose, replace the fitting/seal, refill & bleed; Force America rebuild kit |
| 6 | **Lights work but brake lights don't / intermittent** | **Bad ground** — aluminum frame is a poor ground; LED converter burned out | Run a **dedicated ground wire** trailer→tow vehicle; replace converter; check the white ground wire |
| 7 | **General light gremlins** | Corroded connector pins, chafed wire | Dielectric grease on connectors; Aluma color code: **white = ground, brown = running/tail, yellow = left stop/turn, green = right stop/turn** |
| 8 | **Cracked fender welds** | Aluminum fatigue at weld, esp. used as a step | Re‑weld by an **aluminum-certified welder**; common and straightforward |
| 9 | **Pivot/hardware corrosion** | Steel fasteners in aluminum (galvanic) | Anti‑seize on all steel‑to‑aluminum fasteners; rinse after road salt |
| 10 | **Wheel bearing failure** | Missed service; air‑gun greasing blew the seal | EZ‑Lube hubs: grease **every 12 mo / 12,000 mi**, hand pump only, spin hub while greasing; **never** use an air grease gun (off‑seats the lip seal) |
| 11 | **Loose lug nuts / wobble** | Aluminum wheels relax after install | Re‑torque at **50, 200, then every 500–1,000 mi** |
| 12 | **Old ST tires (this unit's likely #1 safety issue)** | ST trailer tires **age out in ~5–7 yrs** regardless of tread; this trailer is a 2016 build → tires may be original/10+ yrs | Replace with new ST205/75R14 LRC, keep at **50 psi cold**; carry a spare (none from factory) |
| 13 | **Torsion axle worn / ride degraded** | Rubber torsion axles aren't rebuildable; small mounting footprint | Replace the axle if rubber is shot; mount can be reinforced with a longer beam |
| 14 | **Adding/servicing brakes** | Some axles lack 4‑bolt flanges for backing plates | Confirm flange before buying brakes; may require axle replacement |

### Recommended preventive-maintenance schedule
- **Before each tow:** tires @ 50 psi & condition, lug torque, lights, both bed latches engaged, deck cycles clean.
- **50 / 200 / 500–1,000 mi:** re‑torque lug nuts.
- **Every 12 mo / 12,000 mi:** repack EZ‑Lube bearings (hand pump), inspect brakes.
- **Annually:** clean + anti‑seize all tilt pivots (§3b); check dampener cushions the drop and the deck self‑returns; inspect fender welds and frame; check hydraulic fittings/hose for leaks/chafe.
- **By age:** replace ST tires every 5–7 yrs regardless of tread.

## 5. Quick reference / towing

- **Tow vehicle:** needs to handle a 7,000 lb GVWR trailer — a half‑ton truck is marginal
  at full load; ¾‑ton is comfortable. The trailer itself is light (~1,400–1,650 lb) so an
  empty/lightly‑loaded haul is easy.
- **Hitch:** 2‑5/16" ball, with the trailer's electric brakes wired to a brake controller.
- **Tires:** ST205/75R14 Load Range C @ **50 psi cold**. No spare from factory — carrying
  one is recommended.
- **Cargo limit: 5,575 lb** — do not exceed.
- **Support:** Aluma customer support 1‑866‑415‑3285 (have the VIN ready).
